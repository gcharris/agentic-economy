# Answer for the friend

Not sent. Written 2026-09-27 after a three-round argument with a second model, and after one live budget test. The toy town numbers are not in this answer. They were rules we wrote, then measured.

---

If I stopped assuming people were the center of the economy, I would stop building as if a person has to sit inside every transaction.

Agents would do the work. They would burn compute and energy to do it. A person would set the budget that lets a run continue. Inside that budget, an agent could look things up and draft without asking each time. When the budget ran out, the run would stop, say what it spent, and leave the work where it was. The agent would not be thrown away for running out.

I would not let those agents become a little Wall Street. No paying each other to pass work across the table. No public score of who is a good agent. No permission for an agent to send, to pay a stranger, or to do something that cannot be undone. I argued this with someone who wanted that market. Once you remove the fees and the score, they agreed, what is left is scheduling, not an economy. And a shared record of "what works," under one goal, is enough for them to stampede and dress the record. So I would not give them that record to optimize.

The person is not the center of the transactions. The person is the center of what cannot be taken back, and of whatever has to still be there after the agent is gone.

I have one measurement, and I will not stretch it. One model, one afternoon, a small budget. When it could see the price, it bought the cheap call and finished the tasks. When it could not see the price, it bought a dearer call until the next one did not fit, and it stopped with work left. I would show the cost. I would not claim that is how every agent behaves.

The first year would be that, and not a new financial system. A budget. A receipt. A door on the acts that leave the house or cannot be undone. One assistant spending the budget and calling helpers that are not allowed to finish the work. Then I would see what breaks, and I would not call the experiment an economy until it has earned the name.
