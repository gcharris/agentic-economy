I now accept the pressure. A global reputation ledger is a score, and agents will optimize it. Audit-after-the-fact is not control; it is forensics. Escrow only works when the act can be unwound, and many economic acts cannot. So I withdraw the first-year sandbox as originally stated. I no longer defend reputation, agent-to-agent fees, or agent-initiated irreversible closes as first-year components.

If you drop those three, what remains is not an agentic economy in the strong sense. It is a pre-funded, revocable, closed-pool allocation system. Agents may buy compute, bid for capacity, move budget among themselves, issue holds, draft, and reallocate without per-action approval. But they cannot send, pay a stranger, or commit a non-unwindable act unless that class was explicitly pre-authorized and reversible. The person owns the irreversible boundary. I concede that. The one-model price test does not earn the close.

I still refuse the conclusion that this is only a dressed-up tool budget. There is a real difference between a tool and a governed internal market. In a tool budget, the person approves or defines each task, and the tool executes it. In the proposed system, the person sets the envelope and the exit rules, but agents make binding reversible choices inside the envelope: which provider to buy from, which draft to fund, which other agent to allocate compute to, which bid to accept, which project to pause. Those choices are not pre-specified by the person. They are made under scarcity and can be reversed or challenged before the human close. That is a limited economy of revocable claims, not a full legal economy.

So my position now is: the first-year sandbox should be a closed, revocable, human-exitable economy. No global reputation, no uncontrolled payments, no non-reversible close by agents. The person owns only the acts that cannot be unwound. That is a real constraint, but it does not reduce agents to tools. It makes them accountable delegates inside a governed market.

It is not the original agentic economy. I accept that. But it is not merely a burn allowance either, because the allocation decisions are agent-initiated, competitive, and reversible. The human owns the exit; the agents own the movement inside the room.
