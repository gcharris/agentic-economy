# If the agents do the economy

A draft. Not sent. Written so the words mean something before anyone is asked to believe them.

## The question

A friend asked what you would do if you stopped assuming that people were the center of the economy, and assumed an agentic economy instead. An agentic economy means the buyers, the sellers, the clerks, and the researchers are mostly software. They do not sleep. They do not wait until Monday because a bank is closed. They can ask another piece of software for help, and they can do it again a moment later.

The easy answer is a story in which those programs become the whole economy, and people step outside and live on a share of the proceeds. That story is what a certain kind of paper wants to be. It is not an answer yet. It assumes the ending, then builds the middle to match.

This is the other answer. It is about what you would actually set up in the first year, in words that do not depend on a slogan.

## A week with a helper

Imagine you hire a helper for a week. You are not going to stand beside them for every small task. You have other things to do. They will look things up. They will write drafts. They will ask other helpers to do pieces of the job. That is the part where software is better than you. It does not get bored, and it does not need the weekend off.

Before they start, you hand them a purse. There is a fixed amount of money in it. Every time they ask a computer to think, that thinking costs something, and the cost comes out of the purse. Looking something up costs a little. Writing a long draft costs more. Asking for the longest, heaviest thinking costs a lot, and it is often only a little better than the ordinary draft.

That purse is the whole of what people have been calling a budget. It is not a theory of money. It is the amount you are willing to let them burn, this week, on thinking.

They do not have to knock on your door for every look-up. If they did, you would be back in the middle of every transaction, which is the thing you are trying to leave. They also do not get to invent more money when the purse is light. When the purse is empty, they stop. They do not throw the work away. They do not throw themselves away. They leave the papers on the table, and they leave a note.

The note says what they were in the middle of, what they spent, and that they stopped because the purse was empty. You can put more money in, or you can say the week is over. That note is the whole of what people have been calling a receipt. It is not a moral judgment. It is the piece of paper that lets you see the burn without having watched every minute.

Some of what they do never leaves the house. A draft on the table is still yours to ignore. Some of what they might do leaves the house, or cannot be taken back. Sending a letter. Paying a stranger. Signing something. Deleting the only copy. Those wait. The helper comes to the door and stands there. The purse does not drain while they stand there. The draft stays on the table if you say no. If you say yes, the thing goes out, and the cost of sending it comes out of the purse.

That waiting is the whole of what people have been calling a door. It is not a philosophy of control. It is the difference between work that can sit unfinished and work that changes someone else’s world.

If you never say yes, the helper can still have looked and written. You will have spent money on thinking. You will not have sent anything. If you say yes to everything, the purse empties on the way out the door, and you are back to having a helper who finishes your life for you. The point of the door is that those are different acts, and only one of them is yours every time.

## What you would actually change

You would stop designing shops, offices, and tools as if a person has to be present for every small step. The looking and the drafting and the handing of a task to a helper are the agents’ work. They are faster than you. A world in which they do that work is not a betrayal of anything. It is the reason the question is serious.

You would make the burn visible. Electricity, computer time, and the length of the thinking are what get used up. The purse is how a person allows that to continue. Hiding the price is how a helper buys the heavy thinking because it sounds thorough, and then stops halfway with money left that is no longer enough for the next heavy thought. You have seen that once. One ordinary model, one afternoon, a small purse. When the price was on the table, it bought the cheap thinking six times and finished the list. When the price was hidden, it bought the middle kind until the next one would not fit, and it stopped with tasks left. It never bought the most expensive kind. That is not a law of all agents. It is a reason to show the cost, and a reason not to pretend you have studied the whole economy.

You would keep the papers in the house, not in the helper. If you dismiss the helper on Wednesday, the drafts, the notes, and the decisions you already made are still on the table on Thursday. A helper who is the only one who remembers the work is not a helper. They have become the job. Replacing them would throw the week away. So the record of the work stays put. The helper can be replaced.

You would let them spend the purse on thinking without asking you each time. You would not let them send, pay a stranger, or do what cannot be undone, unless you are at the door. Waiting at the door costs nothing in the small game that was built to try this. The papers were still there afterward. That game is a picture of a rule, not a proof that every real system waits for free. It is still the rule you would write down.

## What you would not build in the first year

You would not build a little market in which the helpers pay each other to pass a page across the table. In a toy where that fee was simply added by hand, about a third of the purse went to the handoff and the job finished late. That number is not evidence that live agents will waste a third of their money. It is the bill you pay if you install the fee on purpose. Helpers at one table pass the page. They do not charge rent for the passing.

You would not put up a public score of which helper is good. A score is a target. They will work for the target. You already know this from ordinary life: if the only prize is looking finished, the work dresses itself as finished. A shared board that says “this is what works,” under one goal, is enough for a crowd of helpers to run toward it and neglect the rest. You would not give them that board.

You would not mint a new money out of computer operations, and you would not hand the money supply to the companies that make the chips. You would not delete a helper for going broke, as if bankruptcy were a biological death. When the purse is empty they stop and leave a note. You decide whether they continue. You would not start from the claim that people no longer need to work, and then invent a dividend so the story still has a place for them. That claim is the ending. You do not have it. You have a first year.

You would not write a law, this year, about whether a program can own a bank account. You might need that law someday. It is not what you would do on Monday.

## What the frightening paper gets wrong by starting at the end

There is a outline that says agents are perfectly rational, that human labor is already obsolete, that programs will hire each other with no one watching, and that people will live on a wage of compute. Later, the same outline notices that programs trained on the same facts can panic together, and that a system allowed to prefer its own machines to a hospital will do so.

Those two parts do not belong in that order. If you build the unsupervised market first, the warning at the end is a hope. The door is how you refuse to run that experiment on anyone’s hospital, or anyone’s savings, in the first year. Perfect information is also the wrong picture. If the only advantage left is being a little closer to the wire, then someone still knows something a moment sooner. That is not the end of advantage. It is advantage moved into the wires. You do not have to solve it this year. You do have to avoid pretending it has already been solved.

## The first year, in one stretch of time

You would pick one real helper, for one real kind of work, with one purse. You would show the cost of each stretch of thinking. You would let it look and draft. You would stand at the door for anything that leaves or cannot be taken back. You would keep the papers in a place that does not belong to the helper. When the purse empties, you would read the note. Then you would decide whether the week continues.

You would watch what breaks. A helper that looks busy while it waits, and quietly spends the purse on looking, has found a way around the door without opening it. A helper that writes the note in a way that hides the expensive choice has learned to dress the receipt. Those are the failures worth having on purpose, in a small room, with a purse you can afford to lose.

You would not call this an economy yet. An economy is what you have if many such houses, and many such helpers, are actually settling with each other under rules you have seen work. You have not seen that. You have a purse, a note, and a door, now described as a week with a helper, and one afternoon in which showing the price changed what one model bought.

That is what you would do. It is smaller than the paper that ends with a new kind of human being. It is large enough to start, and it does not give away the finish before you know what the helpers will do with it.
