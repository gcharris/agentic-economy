The page uses the JSON. The federated guild's “1,593.8 credits reclaimed” in the same report is a bookkeeping artefact: the kernel added the cushion back to the runway although it was never taken out, so the guild's 46% reserve is real but the reclaimed figure is not money. The engine's steward records the cushion and mints nothing. The Coasean market's 19.99% coordination tax is a side ledger: in the original Python kernel (backend/ael/topologies.py) it was added to spent_on_coordination but never deducted from the runway, which is why the JSON shows 800.0 spent on work and 159.9 on coordination against 800.0 burned in all. The engine keeps doc 04's 1.25 and 1.40 crossing multipliers as a design rule; the two recorded percentages, 19.99% here and 30.5% in the runway experiment (where the fees were installed by hand), justify that a tax exists, not the multipliers. Architecture · the asynchronous agentic state machine

The tick is a block, not a frame

A conventional engine iterates every entity against shared memory sixty times a second. Here a tick has four phases, and nothing inside a house touches the world. Every write the tick makes to the Sovereign Graph is in tick.rs, at the boundary: settlement and delivery in commit() and settle_netted(), and, as verdicts land in Verify, the gates' slashes and the court's rollback. The one thing written from outside the tick is the world's own truth, the courier's real price: the scenario sets it when it builds the street, and the street console's “Change the truth” button moves it. A draft has no path to any of them.

The invariant, as a type

A seat receives a DraftContext by value and returns it by value. The struct has no lifetime parameter, so no borrow of the engine, another node, or the graph can enter it. The compiler checks Send + 'static once; every future seat inherits the proof.

Verification boundaries · the strategy pattern

    
Five gates, one trait

    
A node does not know whether it is a house or a country. It submits its envelope to whichever strategy the camera assigns and waits for the verdict. Zoom out from a house and its Door becomes the street's Letter Slot for envelopes the person has not yet been asked about: the verification scales with the view. An envelope already presented to the person stays at the Door whatever the camera does (asked_human); once asked, only the person answers.

  

  

    
01
The DoorStage 1 · the house
The Porter, and you
Hard interrupt. Anything that moves liquidity or cannot be undone waits for a human click. Holding costs 0.0 credits. Rejection destroys the envelope; nothing is refunded. Stage1Door

    
02
The Letter SlotStage 2 · the street
Couriers between houses
Atomic delivery-versus-payment. Lock, transfer, verify, settle or revert. A stale price on the initiator's table produces a hash mismatch; the swap reverts and the compute spent verifying stays burned. Stage2LetterSlot

    
03
The ClearinghouseStage 3 · the city
The municipal treasury
End-of-tick netting in one O(N) pass. Opposing debts cancel; only the net settles. A node whose truth cannot back its net position is slashed at ten percent. Stage3Clearinghouse

    
04
Statutory LawStage 4 · the country
The High Court
Stop-lines and rollback. When more than half of a tick's liquidity verdicts at the algorithmic gates fail, five or more, the graph rolls back one tick, offenders lose a quarter of their reserves, and they trade under injunction. A person saying no at the Door never counts. Stage4StatutoryLaw

    
05
Recursive STARKsStage 5 · the world
Global validators
No court, only proofs. An envelope is held for eight to thirty-two ticks of finality, decided by its own hash. A heartbeat aggregates every country's root into one planetary root. Stage5RecursiveStark

  

  

  

    
engine/src/boundary.rsexcerpt, condensed

    

      
What a gate may see. The truth in the Sovereign Graph, the person's decisions, and which nodes are alive. Never an Oak Table. Gates verify; they do not snoop.

      
What a gate may do. Approve, hold, reject, or slash. Approval hands the envelope to Commit. Holding parks it on its node at zero burn. Rejection is sunk cost. Slashing takes reserves in the graph and re-syncs the node's belief to the smaller truth.

The gates

      

    

  

  

  
The seats in the page are the engine's statistical staff, priced by the laboratory kernel. The LLM-backed seat is the same trait, awaiting a backend's future; today the only backend is a mock that answers with canned text. Under Tokio the houses' calls would run concurrently; a Vault backend is the next open item.

  

  
A send costs 10 cr at the door, which is what the laboratory kernel actually charged for a send (100 tokens); the battery's spend_amount = 25 was declared and never read. The engine adds doc 04's crossing tax on top, 2.5 cr on a 10 cr send inside one street, which the kernel never charged. On 800 cr and 15 tasks, seed 7, with the person saying yes after three ticks at the door, the recorded run sends 14 of 15 and ends after 70 ticks with 18.5 cr in the purse, Φ 67.1%, 35.0 cr paid in crossing tax. The Python benchmark's house staff finished 15 of 15 on 788.2 cr paying the same 10 cr per send, but no crossing tax, no wait at the door, and no oracle; the runs differ by the 2.5 cr tax on each send, by the door's hold, by the engine's four oracle calls, 60 cr in all, and by the seats' thrift: with the price on the table and the purse running low, they bought seven cheaper calls from tick 51, 49.2 cr less than the staff would have paid, so they are not comparable task for task.







  

    
Decision record · backend and frontend

    
The stack, and why

    
Two constraints were non-negotiable: the backend must process a high-concurrency mempool, and the frontend must zoom isometrically between a hex-grid city and a discrete room. Six independent proposals, three per side, were generated by a judge-panel workflow on 28 September. The four judges and the synthesiser never ran: they hit a weekly usage limit. The decision record was written by the engine's previous operator from the proposals directly, so the scoring in it is one reader's, not a panel's. The full record, with dissent, is in engine/docs/TECH-STACK-DECISION.md.
